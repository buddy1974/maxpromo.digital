# Competitive Intelligence and Acquisition Architecture

**Date:** 2026-09-18 · **Status:** research, nothing implemented · **Decides:** nothing

This document is research and a proposal. No part of it has been built, and no
part of it should be built until Marcel has decided what enters the roadmap.
The public site was not touched to produce it.

A note on how to read the findings. Everything marked **Observed** was read on
a live page on the date given and is in the source register at the end.
Everything marked **Inference** is my reasoning on top of that, and can be
wrong. Where a number comes from a marketing blog rather than primary
research, it says so, because a statistic with no method behind it is a claim,
not evidence.

---

## 1. Executive finding

The strongest operators do not win because their websites are better. They win
because something useful of theirs is in circulation before anyone needs them.

That is the whole finding. Every mechanism below is a variation on it.

A business owner with a problem does not begin by looking for a supplier. They
begin by looking for an explanation. The operators who do well have already
written the explanation, and their name is on it. By the time the buyer is
ready to talk, the supplier has been chosen, and the conversation is a
formality. Industry surveys put the anonymous part of a B2B purchase at
roughly 60 to 70 percent of the decision, across something like fifteen pieces
of content, before the first contact (marketing-industry figures, not primary
research, see §46).

Maxpromo currently has the opposite shape. The homepage is good and the
company is invisible until someone arrives at it. There is nothing of
Maxpromo's in circulation. Six articles are published, all about Joomla and
WordPress rescue, which is not what the company now says it does. There is a
complete newsletter system with no signup form anywhere on the site. The
capability is real and none of it is reachable.

So the honest answer to the primary question is not "add a funnel". It is:
**Maxpromo has been building the thing that gets found and has not yet built
the thing that does the finding.**

The second finding matters almost as much, and it came out of the German
research rather than the creator research.

For the concrete operational searches a German business owner actually makes,
such as automating quotes or invoices, the results are dominated by **vertical
software vendors**, not by automation agencies. openHandwerk, Bosch OfficeOn,
WinWorker, mfr, Hawepro, Craftivo, easyfirma. Competing there means competing
with funded product companies for a search where the searcher wants to buy
software.

The space nobody owns is the one Maxpromo's homepage already describes: the
business that **already bought the software** and finds the pieces do not talk
to each other. German has a settled word for this, **Insellösung**, island
solution. It has glossary pages, vendor blog posts and real search behaviour
behind it. Maxpromo's own copy is one word away from it. The English homepage
says tools behave "like separate islands" and the German says "getrennte
Inseln", and neither uses the term a German buyer would actually type.

That is the gap. Not "AI automation", which is crowded and generic, but the
work of connecting what a business already runs on.

## 2. The patterns that repeat

Sixteen sources in, the same mechanisms kept appearing. These are the ones
that showed up across unrelated operators, which is the signal that they are
structural rather than personal style.

**1. One free thing with a name.** Not a library. One named artefact. Nick
Saraev has "the 110-step roadmap". Jeff Su has "the AI Toolkit". Dan Martell
has "the Martell Method". A named thing can be referred to, linked, and asked
for. A "resources page" cannot.

**2. The promise states a unit of effort, not a topic.** Jeff Su: "one
actionable AI tip every week (and nothing else)", applicable "in under 2
minutes". Dan Martell: "A 5 Minute Email That Could Save You 5 years". Both
tell you what it costs you to accept. Nobody successful writes "subscribe to
our newsletter".

**3. The free thing is the same skill as the paid thing, at smaller scale.**
Nick Saraev gives away automation blueprints and sells an automation
community. Jeff Su gives away templates and sells template-based courses. The
free item is not a teaser. It is a small complete instance of the work, which
is why it proves competence instead of claiming it.

**4. Discovery happens off the website.** YouTube, community, search, GitHub.
The website is where the visit is converted, not where it originates. Nate
Herk has a million YouTube subscribers and a free community of about 450,000
people; his website is a thin hub. Treating the website as the acquisition
system is the mistake.

**5. The library is the search surface.** n8n's template library claims over
12,000 workflow templates, organised by task and by the tool you already use.
Each is its own page answering one specific question. That is not content
marketing, it is a landing page per intent, generated by genuine utility.

**6. Capture is tied to a specific outcome, never to the brand.** Jeff Su's
lead magnets are free multi-part email courses, each attached to one tool or
one task, each delivering templates. The email address buys a defined thing.

**7. Proof is a by-product of delivery, not a marketing exercise.** The
operators with the most credible proof are the ones whose completed work
automatically produces the next piece of material.

**8. The person is visible and is not the product.** Founder visibility earns
trust; founder worship becomes the business, which is a different company.

**9. The honest local page beats the city-swapped one, and both exist.** The
German market shows the contrast cleanly enough to be a lesson in itself,
below.

## 3. Creator-led businesses

### Nick Saraev (deep dive)

**Observed, 2026-09-18.** nicksaraev.com leads with "Your straight-line path
to a thriving AI & automation business" and a single offer: a free 110-step
roadmap to scaling an automation agency to $25K/month, in exchange for an
email. The roadmap CTA appears twice in the navigation. Products are Maker
School and LeftClick. Content categories are Building in Public, Agency
Strategy, Futurology, Life Improvement.

**Observed.** The Maker School community on Skool shows 2.5k members at $184
per month, described as "the fastest & most affordable way to get client #1
for AI automation with tools like Claude Code, Codex, n8n and more", with a
guarantee of "your first paying AI client in 90d or your money back", 218
videos and 2 admins. A second community, Make Money With Make.com, shows 377
members at $368 per month, "All members vetted".

**Observed.** The Maker School page on his own site carries no price, no
testimonials and no guarantee. Its only CTA is the free roadmap. The selling
happens on Skool.

**Inference.** The structure is deliberate and worth copying at the level of
shape. His own site does one job, which is to convert a stranger into an email
address using a single named artefact. It does not try to sell. The commercial
layer lives on a platform built for it. Two tiers exist at very different
prices for different stages of buyer.

**Inference, and this is the part that transfers.** The roadmap works because
it is the *plan* for the thing his customers want, given away whole. It is not
a sample. Reading it tells you the work is real and also tells you how much of
it there is, which is what makes people buy help rather than do it themselves.

**What Maxpromo can take.** The single named artefact, given away whole,
describing the work honestly. What Maxpromo must not take: the income promise,
the agency-building audience, and the course-and-community business model. Nick
Saraev's customers want to *become* an automation business. Maxpromo's
customers want to *stop doing* manual work. Those are opposite people, and the
material that attracts one repels the other.

### Jeff Su (deep dive)

**Observed, 2026-09-18.** jeffsu.org is organised around tasks and tools, not
around him. Navigation is Home, Newsletter, Courses, Articles, Resources, with
articles split into AI, Productivity, Job Search, Career. The newsletter
promise is exact: "Receive one actionable AI tip every week (and nothing
else)" and "Every week, you'll get 1 insanely practical AI tip you can apply in
under 2 minutes for the tools you already use", with "Join 130K+ readers". Past
issues are browsable and tagged by tool (#ChatGPT, #Claude, #Gemini, #Chrome,
#Gmail).

**Observed.** The lead magnets are free email courses, one per domain: an AI
Toolkit, a Cowork Toolkit (7 days), a Workspace Toolkit, a Notion Toolkit, a
Job Search Toolkit. The AI Toolkit page is headed "For professionals who use AI
but suspect they're only scratching the surface" and promises "A free email
course from an ex-Googler who went through the trial-and-error so you don't
have to", under 5 minutes a day, button text "Get Instant Access to the AI
Toolkit". Subscribers also get first access to quarterly live workshops.

**Inference.** This is the cleanest model in the benchmark group and the most
transferable, because the mechanism is not personality. Three things do the
work. The promise names the effort ("under 2 minutes", "under 5 minutes a
day"). The organising principle is the reader's task and the reader's existing
tools, not his own service list. And the archive is a browsable, tagged,
indexable body of small answers, so each issue keeps earning after it is sent.

**The answer to Marcel's question in §8.** Yes, Maxpromo could produce
comparably useful operational knowledge without becoming a productivity blog,
and the difference is the subject. Jeff Su writes tips for an individual
using a tool. Maxpromo would write about *a process inside a business* and the
handful of people it touches. "How a quote gets from the enquiry to the
customer without being typed twice" is not a productivity tip, it is an
operations note, and no productivity blog will write it.

### Dan Martell

**Observed, 2026-09-18.** danmartell.com leads with "Humanity's Biggest
Cheerleader" and "You have what it takes. Stop playing small and start building
your dream empire." The newsletter is "The Martell Method", pitched as "A 5
Minute Email That Could Save You 5 years". Proof shown is a $100M business,
"12M+ entrepreneurs" reached, the book *Buy Back Your Time*, and speaking
alongside Tony Robbins and John Maxwell. Commercial layer is coaching (Elite
and Quantum Inner Circles), the book, and Martell Ventures.

**Inference.** Almost all of the presentation is wrong for Maxpromo and Marcel
already said so. Two structural things are worth keeping.

The first is the book as the trust anchor. A single durable artefact that
outlives every campaign, is referred to by other people, and does the
credibility work in one line. Maxpromo has no equivalent and the closest
honest version is not a book, it is a body of build notes that a technical
reader can check.

The second is the time-denominated newsletter promise. "5 minutes to save 5
years" is the same device as Jeff Su's "under 2 minutes". Both make the cost of
attention explicit. That is adoptable without the empire language.

### Nate Herk

**Observed, 2026-09-18.** Roughly 1,000,000 YouTube subscribers. Runs AI
Automation Society, a **free** community reported at 450,000+ members, whose
free tier includes 100+ importable n8n templates, Claude Code skills, a 7-day
build challenge with a certificate, and full GitHub repos. A paid "Plus" tier
adds courses, a masterclass, weekly live calls and support.

**Inference.** This is the most extreme version of pattern 3. The free tier is
so substantial that it is the product, and the paid tier sells access and
pace rather than information. For Maxpromo this is instructive as a limit
case rather than a model: it only works at a scale of audience Maxpromo does
not have and should not chase. The transferable part is narrower and real: a
**7-day build challenge** is a lead magnet with a completion state, and
completion is what turns a subscriber into someone who trusts you.

## 4. AI and automation companies

The most useful commercial operator found is German and appears in §5, because
its lessons are inseparable from its market.

The general observation across the commercial operators, as distinct from the
creators: **the creators sell knowledge and therefore give away knowledge; the
companies sell implementation and therefore give away diagnosis.** A company
that sells implementation does not need a template library. It needs the
visitor to arrive already able to describe their own problem accurately. That
is a different free artefact, and it points directly at the Business Friction
Check.

## 5. German market

This was the most productive part of the research, and it changed two
recommendations.

### Who appears

**Observed, 2026-09-18**, for "KI Automatisierung Agentur Essen NRW
Prozessautomatisierung Mittelstand": optikronix.de, bonuski.de,
bergisch-digital-agentur.de, franklin-partners.de, dein-copilot.de, kibrains.de,
foxifai.com, seele.gmbh.

**Observed.** Six of the eight use a city slug in the URL. The patterns are
`/ki-agentur-essen`, `/ki-beratung-essen`, `/ki-agentur/essen`,
`/ki-beratung-essen.html`, and, most explicitly,
`bergisch-digital-agentur.de/staedte/nordrhein-westfalen/essen/ki-automatisierung-essen/`,
a literal `/staedte/` (cities) directory.

**Observed.** That Bergisch Digital city URL now returns **HTTP 410 Gone**. 410
is a deliberate, permanent removal, not a broken link.

**Inference.** Somebody built a programmatic city-page tree and then
deliberately destroyed at least part of it. 410 rather than 404 means a human
decided those URLs should never come back. That is the clearest available
evidence that the city-page strategy has a downside its own practitioners have
run into.

### The doorway end of the market

**Observed, kibrains.de/ki-agentur-essen/.** Headline "KI Agentur Essen:
Energiewirtschaft, Handel, Industrie". "Essen" appears over 60 times. No
physical address. RWE, Aldi, ThyssenKrupp and E.ON are named as regional
context and are not clients. Statistics are unattributed ("700+ Projekte", "50%
Zeitersparnis"). Prices are shown as "ab 5.000 €" for pilots and a
10,000 to 150,000 € range. Two team names, no photographs, no stated connection
to Essen. A "Digitalisierungsrechner" is offered. The footer links the same
page structure for Dortmund, Bochum and Duisburg.

**Inference.** This is a template with a city inserted. Maxpromo must not build
this, and Marcel already said so. Two mechanisms are still worth noting even
here, because a bad page can contain a good device: a **calculator** as the
free artefact, and **visible price ranges**. Maxpromo currently shows neither.

### The honest end of the market, and the strongest competitor found

**Observed, foxifai.com/ki-agentur-essen.** Headline: "KI Agentur für Essen:
FoxifAI automatisiert Workflows, Angebote und Kundenkommunikation". The named
processes are concrete and specific: automatic quote generation from enquiry to
formatted PDF, invoice processing and dunning via **Lexware and sevDesk**, lead
capture from website, email and phone into one CRM, email classification and
assignment to staff, appointment confirmations and reminders via Google
Calendar and Outlook.

**Observed.** One case study, a cleaning firm of eight people in
Essen-Rüttenscheid; the owner spent two hours a day on admin and could not take
new clients despite demand; afterwards they added several regular customers
without hiring. Prices are stated: from €1,920 setup plus €100 per month,
annually cancellable. CTA is "Jetzt kostenloses Erstgespräch buchen" via
cal.com, repeated. No founder or team is visible anywhere. The FAQ states all
workflows run on the company's own n8n instances on **Hetzner servers in
Germany**, with a data processing agreement on request. The footer still
reveals city-swapped siblings for Hamm, Bochum and Dortmund.

**Observed, the strongest single line on any page in this research:** "Die
Kapazitätsgrenze hatte nie am Personal gelegen, sondern am Backoffice." The
capacity ceiling was never the staff, it was the back office.

**Inference, and this is the competitive read.** FoxifAI is doing what Maxpromo
says it does, in Maxpromo's city, at a published price, with German tooling and
German hosting, and one honest local story. They beat Maxpromo today on
concreteness, on price transparency and on data-residency reassurance. Maxpromo
beats them on exactly one thing, and it is not small: **they have no human on
the site at all.** Marcel is a named, experienced, locatable person in Essen.
That is the one asset a templated competitor cannot copy quickly.

### What German buyers actually search

**Observed.** For concrete operational problems ("Rechnungen automatisch
erfassen", "Angebote automatisch erstellen", "Daten doppelt eingeben" in a
trades context) the results are almost entirely **vertical software products**:
openHandwerk, Bosch OfficeOn, WinWorker, mfr, Hawepro, Craftivo, easyfirma,
desk4. Their own copy uses the exact pain language: data has to be entered
twice, a quote should flow into an order and then into an invoice.

**Observed.** For the integration problem the German term of art is
**Insellösung**. Searching it returns dictionary and glossary entries (DWDS,
anaptis, humbee) and vendor explainers (VARIO, GSD, Sartissohn, WSK,
digital-zolutions). The described symptom is exact: separate systems for
warehouse, accounting, CRM, shop middleware and support, each fine alone,
together "ein komplexes, fehleranfälliges Netz aus Daten, Schnittstellen und
Abstimmungsaufwand". The named remedy is **Systemintegration** and
**Schnittstellen**.

**Inference.** Two conclusions, and they are the most actionable findings in
this document.

First, **do not compete on "automate invoices"**. That search belongs to
software vendors with product teams and budgets, and the searcher is shopping
for a product.

Second, **Insellösung and Systemintegration are Maxpromo's words and Maxpromo
is not using them.** The homepage already describes this exact problem in both
languages and uses a metaphor ("getrennte Inseln") that is one step from the
term a German buyer would type. That is a rare position: the right idea already
written, in the wrong vocabulary. Changing it is a copy decision, not a build.

## 6. Open source and GitHub

**Observed, 2026-09-18, github.com/n8n-io/n8n.** 205.2k stars, 60.8k forks.
Tagline: "Fair-code workflow automation platform with native AI capabilities.
Combine visual building with custom code, self-host or cloud, 400+
integrations." README opens with what it is and what it connects to, then goes
straight to a one-line quick start: `curl -fsSL https://get.n8n.io | sh`,
editor at localhost:5678. README order: Key Capabilities, Quick Start,
Resources, Support, License, Contributing, Join the Team, What does n8n mean,
About. Support is a community forum. Licence is dual, fair-code plus
enterprise.

**Observed, n8n.io/workflows.** The template library states "12405 Workflow
Automation Templates". Organised primarily by **task** (AI, Sales, IT Ops,
Marketing, Document Ops, Support) and secondarily by **the tool you already
have** ("Need inspiration? What's in your stack?"). Templates come from the
company and from verified community creators, who are incentivised through an
affiliate programme.

**Inference. The answer to "why can a stranger discover this, understand it and
start using it?"** is three things, in order, and none of them is the homepage.

The stranger arrives on a *template page*, not a product page, because the
template page is the answer to the thing they searched for. They understand it
because the README states the problem before the architecture. They start
because the quick start is one line and the first success takes minutes.

**What this teaches Maxpromo about making expertise discoverable.** The lesson
is not "build a template library". Maxpromo cannot and should not produce
12,000 of anything, and a consultancy's templates are not its product.

The lesson is the **unit**. n8n's unit of discovery is one specific task, on
its own page, findable by the tool the visitor already owns. Maxpromo's
equivalent unit already exists and is not published: **a real workflow that was
built for a real business, described as a before and an after.** Ten of those,
each on its own page, indexed by the business task and by the tools involved,
is the same machine at the scale Maxpromo actually operates at. Each one is
simultaneously a search surface, a proof item and a sales asset, which is the
efficiency n8n gets from templates.

The second lesson is the **order of the README**: problem, then what it is,
then how to start, then everything else. That order is exactly what the new
homepage does, and it should govern every capability page too.

## 7. What Maxpromo should adopt

Adopt means the mechanism fits nearly as-is.

**A1. One named free artefact, given away whole.** The Business Friction Check
is already the right instinct and already announced. It becomes the single
named thing. Not a resources page, not several lead magnets. One.

**A2. Effort-denominated promises.** Every capture point states what it costs
the visitor in time and what they get. "Eight questions, about four minutes, and
you get a written summary of where the work is piling up." Never "sign up for
updates".

**A3. The unit of publication is one real workflow, before and after.** Taken
from n8n's template pattern and from FoxifAI's single honest case study. One
page per system built, structured as: what the business was doing, what it cost
them, what was changed, what it costs them now, which tools were involved.

**A4. Organise by the visitor's task and the visitor's existing tools.** Jeff
Su and n8n both do this and it is the opposite of organising by service line.
A page that can be found by searching "Lexware" or "Angebot" will be found; a
page called "Workflow Automation" will not.

**A5. Problem before architecture, everywhere.** Already true of the homepage.
Make it the rule for capability pages, articles and the README of anything
published.

**A6. Publish the German vocabulary the market uses.** Insellösung,
Systemintegration, Schnittstellen, Prozessautomatisierung. Not as keywords
sprinkled in, but because they are the correct words for what the company does
and the current copy is using a metaphor instead.

## 8. What Maxpromo should adapt

Adapt means the mechanism is useful but must change to fit.

**B1. Founder visibility: adapt, do not amplify.** Buyers trust people faster
than logos, and the strongest local competitor has no person on the site at
all. So Marcel appears, with a name, a face and a locatable history, on About,
on build notes, on case studies, in the newsletter signature, on LinkedIn. He
does not appear as the subject. The test: he is visible as the person who did
the work, never as the reason to buy.

**B2. Free multi-part email course: adapt the delivery, change the subject.**
Jeff Su's format works because completion builds trust. Maxpromo's version is
not tips about tools. It is a short sequence that walks an owner through
looking at their own operation, and it should be the natural follow-up to the
Friction Check rather than a separate magnet.

**B3. Price transparency: adapt, do not copy the range.** Two German
competitors publish numbers (€1,920 setup plus €100/month; "ab 5.000 €").
Maxpromo publishes a €999 build floor on one page. The adaptation is not to
invent prices, which is forbidden and would be wrong anyway. It is to make the
*shape* of an engagement visible: how it starts, what the first step costs, what
happens next. Marcel confirms any figure.

**B4. The calculator: adapt with a warning.** A manual-work cost calculator is
genuinely useful and shareable. The adaptation is that it must compute from the
visitor's own numbers and show its arithmetic, never produce a score. See §13.

**B5. Community: adapt to almost nothing.** Nate Herk's 450,000-member free
community works at a scale Maxpromo will not reach and should not target. The
only transferable piece is the **completion state**: a thing the visitor
finishes. The Friction Check has one built in.

**B6. Data residency and DSGVO: adapt from competitor practice.** FoxifAI makes
German hosting a headline reassurance. Maxpromo should state its true position
plainly wherever a German SMB is deciding. This is a factual disclosure, not a
claim, and Marcel must confirm what is actually true of the infrastructure
before a word of it is published.

## 9. What Maxpromo should ignore

**C1. City pages.** Six of eight German competitors build them and one has
already issued 410 Gone on theirs. Maxpromo is genuinely in Essen and can say
so honestly on a handful of pages. Building `/ki-automatisierung-dortmund` and
forty siblings would be the single fastest way to make an honest company look
like the templated ones. **Do not build.**

**C2. llms.txt.** The evidence is one-sided. Google's documentation was updated
in June 2026 to state the file has no effect on Search or AI Overviews and is
ignored; Gary Illyes said in July 2025 it is not supported and not planned.
Ahrefs found that across 137,000 domains, 97 percent of llms.txt files received
zero requests in May 2026. SE Ranking found no statistically significant
correlation with AI citation across roughly 300,000 domains. **Do not build**,
and revisit only if a named engine announces support.

**C3. An AI-news or model-commentary surface.** Matthew Berman's model. Perpetual
republishing, no durable asset, and on Marcel's do-not-become list.

**C4. Course and community as a revenue line.** Nick Saraev's and Nate Herk's
model. It attracts people who want to build an automation agency, which is not
a customer, and it would reposition Maxpromo away from implementation.

**C5. Aggressive capture.** Popups on every page, exit intent, forced Calendly,
fake scarcity, countdowns. None of the operators worth respecting here use
them, and they contradict the tone the homepage just established.

**C6. Guru framing.** "Humanity's Biggest Cheerleader" and "$100M empire" are
Dan Martell's and should stay his.

**C7. A score out of 100.** Discussed at §13. "AI readiness: 87%" is a
fabricated number and the company has a guardrail against fabricated numbers.

## 10. Maxpromo acquisition architecture

The smallest coherent system, after the reduction §38 demands. Five parts. Not
Nick's library plus Jeff's newsletter plus Dan's brand.

```
  1. SOMETHING FINDABLE
     A real workflow, written up as before and after, on its own page.
     Found by task, by tool name, or by the German term for the problem.
              |
  2. RECOGNITION
     The visitor sees their own week described accurately.
     This is the homepage's job and it already does it.
              |
  3. SOMETHING USEFUL, FREE, FINISHABLE
     The Business Friction Check. Result first, email optional and after.
              |
  4. PROOF THEY CAN CHECK
     The same workflow write-ups, plus a named person who built them.
              |
  5. A NEXT STEP SIZED TO THE VISITOR
     Not one CTA. Three, matched to how ready they are.
              |
  6. THE WORK ITSELF
              |
     produces the next item at (1), and the loop closes.
```

The loop at the bottom is the part that makes this sustainable for a company of
this size. Maxpromo does not have a content team and will not have one. The
only content strategy that survives contact with reality is one where
**delivering a project produces the marketing material as a by-product**. Every
other model requires someone to sit down and invent things to say.

## 11. Website architecture

Starting from the accepted structure, with a purpose for every page. Nothing
here is proposed to make the sitemap larger.

| Page | Audience | Discovery role | Conversion role | Proof needed | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/` | Owner | brand, direct | recognition, routing | two figures, founder | accepted, do not touch |
| `/what-we-do` | Owner | category search | routes to capability | one example per capability | hub |
| `/what-we-do/<capability>` (5) | Owner | task + tool search | capability-specific CTA | one real system each | commercial |
| `/work` | Owner | low | proof, demo request | the write-ups | hub |

## 13. Business Friction Check

The homepage says COMING NEXT and links nowhere, which was the right call. This
is the concept, not an implementation.

**What it is.** Eight to ten questions about how work moves through the
business, producing a written description of where it piles up. Four minutes.

**What it is not.** It is not a score. There is no percentage, no maturity
level, no "AI readiness 87%". A score invents precision the input cannot
support, and G8-style honesty rules out fabricated numbers. It is also the
single most common way these tools lose credibility with an experienced owner,
who knows perfectly well that ten questions cannot grade their company.

**What it asks.** Questions about observable events, not opinions. Not "how
mature is your automation strategy" but:

- Where do new enquiries arrive? (pick all: phone, email, website form,
  WhatsApp, walk-in, portal)
- After an enquiry arrives, who types its details into something else?
- How many separate systems hold customer information?
- Which of these are produced by hand: quote, order confirmation, invoice,
  reminder, report?
- What has to be chased? (approvals, documents, signatures, payment)
- Roughly how many of these a week?
- What breaks when the person who normally does it is away?
- Which tools are already paid for? (free text plus common German list)

**What it returns, immediately and without an email.** Three things:

1. A plain-language description of their own flow, read back to them. This is
   the whole value. Industry research suggests the supplier who first frames
   the problem accurately tends to win the work (a marketing-industry figure,
   §46, treat as indicative).
2. The two or three places where the same information is being handled more
   than once, named specifically. "Enquiry details are entered in three places"
   is useful. "Your automation maturity is Level 2" is not.
3. An arithmetic estimate, shown with its working, using only numbers the
   visitor supplied. "You said around 40 quotes a month and about 20 minutes of
   typing each. That is roughly 13 hours a month." No benchmark, no industry
   average, no invented saving.

**Email: after, and optional.** The result appears on screen first. Then: "Want
this as a PDF, or a short written note on what we would look at first?" That is
the capture, and refusing it costs the visitor nothing. This is the opposite of
the standard pattern and it is the right one for a company whose homepage just
promised not to play games.

**What it deliberately does not collect.** Revenue, headcount bands, budget,
job title, company size, phone number. None of it improves the answer and all
of it signals that the form is a qualification exercise. Company name should be
optional.

**Where it leads, by what the answers show.**

- Heavy duplicate entry → the integration guide, and the systems write-up that
  matches their tools
- One dominant manual document → the relevant capability page
- Chasing and approvals → a workflow example with a human approval step
- "Nothing much, we are fine" → the honest result says so, and offers nothing.
  A diagnostic that never says "you do not need us" is not a diagnostic.

**Follow-up.** One email, written by a person, referencing what they actually
answered. Not a sequence. The sequence can come later if the thing works.

## 15. Proof system

Only categories Maxpromo can legitimately accumulate. Ordered by how much a
sceptical buyer weights them.

1. **A system write-up with before and after.** Strongest, and Maxpromo can
   produce one after every project. Requires client consent for the situation,
   not the name.
2. **A live private demo of a relevant system.** Strong because it is not
   editable. Current direction is right.
3. **Measured outcomes with their source named.** The homepage does this well
   with two figures. Two honest figures beat five vague ones.
4. **Named client work.** Blocked by agreements today. Should be requested at
   the end of successful projects as a matter of routine, because it is the
   only proof category that cannot be manufactured later.
5. **Build notes and public reasoning.** Credible to technical evaluators and
   to AI answer engines, for the same reason: specific, first-hand, checkable.
6. **The founder's history.** Fifteen years, verifiable, already stated.
7. **Screenshots and diagrams.** Support, never lead.

**Never:** invented clients, borrowed logos, fabricated testimonials, review
counts, or a figure whose source cannot be named. The claims audit already
enforces part of this and currently reports four hedged statements on the Work
page, which should be resolved by Marcel rather than reworded by anyone else.

**The accumulation rule that makes this work:** consent, evidence and figures
are collected **at project close**, while the client is happy and the numbers
are at hand. Retrofitting proof six months later is how companies end up with
none.

## 18. Email and newsletter decision

**Recommendation: LATER, and there is a live problem to fix first.**

This is not the answer I expected to give, and the reason is internal rather
than competitive.

**Observed in the repository, 2026-09-18.** A complete newsletter system
already exists: `apps/web/app/api/newsletter/subscribe/route.ts` with rate

**Observed.** No public page anywhere on the site posts to that endpoint. There

question.

**The conditions for GO**, all three: at least four `/work/<system>` write-ups
published; the Friction Check live, because that is what makes subscribing make
sense; and the promise rewritten to something keepable. Monthly, event-driven,
explicitly not weekly. "When we finish a system, we write up what changed. Once

memory rule says findings go in the documentation: the welcome email, the

because this phase changes no production code.

## 21. Commercial conversion paths

One CTA everywhere is the current pattern and it is wrong, because visitors
arrive at different distances from a decision. Four paths, matched to distance:

**Not ready, just recognising the problem.** → The Friction Check. No email
required to get the result.

**Recognises it, wants to see it work.** → A relevant system write-up, then
"ask to see this one running". This is the demo request, and it should be
specific to what they just read rather than a generic demo button.

**Knows what they want changed.** → "Tell us what is slowing you down." The
current primary CTA, which is correct for this group and only this group.

**Ready to talk.** → Email Marcel directly. A visible address, not only a form.

What to avoid, and Marcel already named most of it: long diagnostic forms
before any value, a forced calendar embed, fake urgency, and a single button
repeated eleven times.

The one addition from the research: **a next step must exist for the visitor
who is not going to buy.** That is what the Friction Check is for, and it is
why the email must be optional. A visitor who leaves with something useful and
no obligation is the one who comes back in four months, which is roughly the
length of the anonymous research phase the B2B surveys describe.

## 24. Impact on the next website phase

The planned next step was to rebuild Solutions and its five capability pages.
The research changes that plan in four specific ways.

**First, the capability pages are not the next thing to build.** They are the
page type with the least discovery value in the whole architecture. Nobody
searches "workflow automation" who is not already shopping for a supplier, and
that visitor is rare and late. `/work/<system>` is where the demand actually
arrives. Building capability pages first means building the destination before
the road.

**Second, each capability page needs a real system to point at, and only one
exists in publishable form.** A capability page whose proof section is empty is
a brochure. The sequence should be: write up systems, then build the capability
page that groups them.

**Third, the naming should be reconsidered against how people search.** The
planned set is Workflow Automation, Custom Applications, Web Development,
Content & Social Operations, Product & Commerce Operations. Four of those five
are internal category names. The German market research says the words in use
are Prozessautomatisierung, Systemintegration, Schnittstellen and
individuelle Software. "Content & Social Operations" in particular corresponds
to nothing a business owner searches for in either language.

**Fourth, and specifically for each planned page**, what should change:

- **Workflow Automation.** Strongest of the five. Lead with the duplicate-entry
  problem, not the word automation. Needs one real system and the German term.
- **Custom Applications.** The trigger is outgrowing a spreadsheet, and the
  page should say so in the first line. Search intent is "individuelle
  Software" and "Excel ersetzen", not "custom application development".
- **Web Development.** Riskiest page in the set, because it invites the
  positioning the company explicitly rejects. It only earns its place if it is
  about a website that does operational work. The existing legacy-CMS articles,
  if kept, belong here and would give it the proof it otherwise lacks.
- **Content & Social Operations.** Weakest fit. Recommend folding it into
  Workflow Automation as a use case until there is a delivered system that
  justifies a page. Marcel's call.
- **Product & Commerce Operations.** Real, but needs at least one commerce
  system written up before it says anything specific. Defer.

**The recommended sequence**, replacing "rebuild Solutions next":

1. One system write-up, by hand, to prove the page type
2. The Friction Check
3. Three more write-ups
4. Then the capability pages, each pointing at real work, with names chosen
   against how people actually search
